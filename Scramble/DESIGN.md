# Scramble — Design

How the code works, and why it is shaped this way.

## Game concept

You fly a jet left-to-right through a winding cavern that scrolls forward on its
own. The cave floor and roof close in, ground batteries fire rockets up at you,
and — the twist that drives every decision — **your fuel is always draining**.
A full tank lasts about 45 seconds; the trip takes about 65. You cannot finish
the run by flying well. You finish it by bombing the fuel dumps on the cave
floor along the way, which means deliberately flying *low*, into the dangerous
part of the cave, at the moments you can least afford it.

Four zones (`CAVERNS`, `PEAKS`, `TUNNEL`, `CITY`) escalate the terrain: rolling
ground, tall peaks, a low roof that squeezes you into the floor, then a mix.

## Mechanics

### The world

Terrain is a **heightmap of 20px columns** — `terrain.ceiling[i]` measured down
from the top, `terrain.ground[i]` measured up from the bottom. This makes
collision a cheap scan of the two or three columns the ship overlaps
(`hitsTerrain`) rather than any polygon work, and it makes the cave trivially
inspectable from tests.

The level is generated by a small LCG (`makeRng`) so it is **deterministic**:
one fixed seed (`LEVEL_SEED`) produces one fixed cave for every run. Each zone
has a profile of min/max floor and roof heights; every 8 columns the generator
picks new targets and the rock eases toward them (`TERRAIN_EASE`), which gives
smooth ridges instead of noise. A final pass guarantees the passage is never
narrower than `MIN_GAP`, so an unflyable cave cannot be generated.

### Fuel — the core loop

`FUEL_RATE` drains 2.2 units/second from a 100-unit tank. Hitting 0 costs a
life. Fuel tanks on the cave floor restore `FUEL_PER_TANK` (22), **but only
when destroyed by a bomb**. Shooting one with the laser scores the same points
and burns the fuel away. That asymmetry is the whole game: the laser is the
safe, horizontal, long-range weapon, and it is the wrong tool for survival.

### Weapons

- **Laser** — flies horizontally at ship height. Good against rockets once they
  have risen into your lane, and against UFOs. Capped at `LASER_MAX` in the air
  with a short cooldown.
- **Bomb** — released forward and down, then accelerated by `BOMB_GRAVITY` into
  a parabola. Bursts on terrain. The lower and slower you fly, the easier the
  arc is to land on a tank.

### Enemies

- **Fuel tank** — inert; a target, not a threat (though you still crash into it).
- **Rocket** — sits on the floor until the ship is within `ROCKET_TRIGGER`
  (210px), then climbs straight up at a constant speed. It occupies one narrow
  column, so it is dodged by changing altitude or shot once it reaches your lane.
- **UFO** — drifts backward through the cave on a sine bob, clamped inside the
  passage. Worth the most points.

Enemies only think while inside `ACTIVE_RANGE` of the camera, so the ~150
entities in the level cost nothing off-screen.

### Lives and checkpoints

A crash costs a life, freezes the sim for `DEATH_DELAY`, then respawns the ship
at `checkpointX` — the start of the furthest zone reached — with a full tank.
Rockets that had launched are reset to the ground; enemies you already destroyed
stay destroyed, so a repeated section gets easier rather than resetting wholesale.

Reaching `FINISH_X` wins the mission and pays `SCORE_FINISH` plus 5 points per
unit of fuel left over — the bonus rewards efficient bombing rather than
hoarding, since fuel you never burn is fuel you flew too cautiously to need.

## Controls

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| `←` `→` `↑` `↓` / `WASD` | Fly (→ outruns the scroll, ← drops back) |
| `Space` | Fire laser (or start the game) |
| `B` / `X` | Drop bomb |
| `P` | Pause / resume |

The ship is dragged forward at `SCROLL_SPEED` and clamped to a screen window of
`[SHIP_MIN_SCREEN, SHIP_MAX_SCREEN]`. Pushing right buys you reaction distance
up to the front of that window but cannot outrun the camera over the long run,
so total flight time is bounded by the scroll — which is what makes the fuel
budget a real constraint rather than a speedrun.

## Code layout

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `index.html` | HUD, fuel gauge, canvas, overlay |
| `style.css` | Dark arcade theme shared in spirit with the other games |
| `game.js` | Generation, simulation, rendering, input |
| `tests/scramble.spec.js` | 70 Playwright tests |

`game.js` deliberately keeps its state and functions at **top-level script
scope** (no IIFE, no modules), matching the other games in this repo. The
Playwright suite drives the simulation directly — `step(0.016)`, `placeShipAt`,
`terrainAt`, `fireLaser` — instead of racing `requestAnimationFrame`, which is
what makes physics, scoring and collision testable without screenshots.

The loop is a plain `requestAnimationFrame` that calls `step(dt)` (dt clamped to
0.05 so an alt-tab cannot teleport the ship through a wall) and then `draw()`.
`step` is a pure-ish simulation tick and `draw` never mutates state, so tests can
step the world any number of frames and only then render.

## Assumptions

These were open questions; the simpler reading was taken in each case and is
recorded here rather than guessed at again later.

1. **Branch name.** The task asked for a branch named after the game
   (`scramble`), but the session's standing instructions designate
   `claude/loving-euler-7z4gna` and forbid pushing elsewhere. The designated
   branch wins; the game name is carried by the folder instead.
2. **One fixed level, not endless.** A single hand-seeded 6400px course with a
   finish line, rather than procedurally endless scrolling. It gives the run a
   win condition, makes the level learnable, and makes tests deterministic.
3. **Only bombs refuel.** Classic Scramble refuels on any hit to a tank. Making
   it bomb-only creates the risk/reward that the fuel timer needs, and it is the
   simpler rule to state and to test.
4. **Fixed-seed generation over authored levels.** Cheaper than hand-authoring
   6400px of terrain, and still perfectly reproducible.
5. **Checkpoints at zone boundaries.** Simpler than continuous checkpointing and
   it keeps the punishment for a crash meaningful without being a full restart.
6. **No sound.** No other game in the repo ships audio, and no asset pipeline
   exists for it.
7. **Desktop keyboard only.** Consistent with the rest of the repo; no touch
   controls.
