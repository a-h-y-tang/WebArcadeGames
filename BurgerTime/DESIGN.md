# Burger Time — Design

## Concept

Burger Time is a single-screen platform arcade game inspired by the 1982 Data
East cabinet. **Chef Pepper** runs along a lattice of girders and ladders that
holds three unfinished burgers. Walking the full width of an ingredient knocks it
down to the next girder; keep knocking pieces down until all four parts of every
burger land on the plates at the bottom and the level is served.

Three food enemies — a hot dog, a pickle and a fried egg — hunt the chef the
whole time. Touching one costs a life. Two answers: a shake of the **pepper pot**
freezes an enemy in place for a few seconds, or you drop an ingredient right on
top of it and squash it flat for 500 points.

## The board

The world is a 20 × 16 grid of 32 px tiles (a 640 × 512 canvas). Seven girders
run across it at rows 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 and 14; the bottom one doubles as the
plate shelf. Ladders always join two neighbouring girders.

```
    0123456789...
 2  .+-----+-----+-----.      '.'  empty
 3  .|.....|.....|......      '-'  girder
 4  .+----++----++----+.      '|'  ladder
 5  ......|.....|.....|.      '+'  girder with a ladder passing through
 6  .+----++----+-----+.
 7  .|.....|..........|.
 8  .+----++-----+----+.
 9  ......|......|....|.
10  .+----++----++----+.
11  .|.....|....|.......
12  .+----++----++----+.
13  ......|......|....|.
14  .-----+------+----+.
```

`MAP` is generated by `buildMap()` from `FLOOR_ROWS` and `LADDER_SEGMENTS` rather
than typed out by hand, so a girder and the ladders that meet it can never drift
out of sync. Ladder columns are chosen from `{1, 6, 7, 12, 13, 18}` — the columns
no burger stack occupies. A ladder underneath an ingredient would be unusable the
moment that ingredient settled on it.

The three burger stacks start at columns 2, 8 and 14 and are four tiles wide.
Each holds a bun top, lettuce, patty and bun bottom, spread across the girders
with deliberate gaps (`STACK_ROWS`) so that a full top-to-plate chain reaction is
something you have to set up rather than something you get for free.

## Mechanics

### Walking an ingredient down

An ingredient is drawn as four segments, one per tile. While the chef stands on a
girder, `stepOnIngredients()` presses down whichever segment he is over. Once all
four are pressed the whole pile on that spot drops one girder.

Piles, not single ingredients, are what fall. Anything already resting where a
falling pile lands is knocked loose too, joins the group *underneath* it, and the
whole column carries on to the next girder — the chain reaction that makes a good
run worth chasing. A knocked-down ingredient has its segments reset, so a piece
that gets shoved down still has to be walked over again on its new girder.

A group that reaches the plate row is *plated*: it stacks up on the plate and is
out of play. Four plated pieces complete a burger; three complete burgers clear
the level.

### Falling groups

`fallingGroups` holds each moving column as `{ stack, members, targetRow, y }`,
where `members` runs bottom-of-pile first and `y` tracks the bottom member. Every
member's own `y` is derived from the group's in `syncGroup()`, which keeps a
falling stack rigid for free. `landGroup()` resolves an arrival in one of three
ways: plate it, absorb what it landed on and keep going, or settle it.

### Enemies

Enemies move node to node across the same girder/ladder graph the chef uses, so
they can never end up somewhere the level does not go. At each node
`chooseEnemyTarget()` lists the legal moves (left, right, up a ladder, down a
ladder), throws out the one it just came from, and takes whichever remaining move
shortens the Manhattan distance to the chef — with a 25% chance of picking at
random instead, which is what stops all three from tracing the same line.

Vertical distance is weighted 1.5× so an enemy prefers to close on the chef's
floor before chasing him along it.

### Pepper

`sprayPepper()` drops a short-lived cloud in front of the chef. Any enemy inside
it is stunned for 3.5 s: it stops dead, becomes harmless to touch, and is worth
100 points. You get five shakes per level and the pot refills when a level is
cleared, so pepper is a resource to spend rather than a panic button.

### Scoring

| Event | Points |
|---|---|
| Each ingredient that falls one girder | 50 |
| Enemy peppered | 100 |
| Enemy squashed by a falling ingredient | 500 |
| Level cleared | 1000 × level |

Because chain reactions score every ingredient for every girder they travel,
setting up a long cascade is worth far more than walking pieces down one at a
time. The best score is kept in `localStorage` under `burgertime-best`.

### Difficulty

Everything scales from `level`: `enemySpeedFor()` runs `46 + 8 × (level-1)` px/s
capped at 92 (the chef walks at a constant 100), and `enemyCountFor()` adds an
enemy every second level up to five. The layout itself stays the same, so a later
level is the same puzzle under more pressure.

## Controls

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| ← / → or A / D | Walk along a girder |
| ↑ / ↓ or W / S | Climb a ladder |
| Space | Shake the pepper pot (start / continue on the overlays) |
| P | Pause / resume |

Climbing needs the chef within 14 px of a ladder's centre; he snaps to it and
`climb()` walks him up or down, stopping at whichever girder ends the shaft. Where
two ladder segments meet on the same girder the climb continues through it, so a
tall shaft is one smooth movement.

## Determinism & testing

`game.js` is a single classic (non-module) script, so every constant, function and
piece of state is a plain global the Playwright tests can reach — the same
approach Kaboom, Dino Run and Tetris take in this repo.

All motion is per-second and advanced through `step(dt)`, which the real
`requestAnimationFrame` loop and the tests both call. A test simulates exact
frames with `for (let i = 0; i < n; i++) step(0.016)` and never waits on
wall-clock time. `step()` returns immediately unless the state is `running`, which
is also what makes pausing work.

Enemy wandering uses a small seeded LCG (`rand()`) re-seeded from the level number
in `resetLevel()`, so a level plays out identically given identical input.

Test hooks worth knowing about:

- `placePlayer(col, row)` / `placeEnemy(e, col, row)` park an actor exactly on a
  grid node, so a test can set up a situation without hand-computing pixels.
- `dropPile(stack, row)` triggers a fall directly, skipping the walk.
- `plateEverything()` serves every burger at once, for the level-clear paths.

The suite in `tests/burgertime.spec.js` covers the map's structure, chef and enemy
movement, the drop/chain/plate rules, pepper, lives and the level cycle. Two tests
are there specifically to keep the level honest if the layout is ever edited:

- *every walkable cell is reachable from the chef spawn* does a breadth-first
  search over the girder/ladder graph, so a burger can never sit somewhere no
  ladder reaches.
- *a scripted playthrough can serve every burger* actually plays the level —
  repeatedly walking the chef across whichever ingredient is still resting until
  the game reports `levelclear` — using only the real mechanics, with no state
  forced.

## Assumptions

These are the judgement calls made while building the game, recorded as the task
asked.

- **Branch name.** The task asked for a branch named after the game
  (`burger-time`). This session is pinned to the branch `claude/loving-euler-xbs0ur`
  and instructed never to push elsewhere, so the work was developed and pushed
  there instead. The branch requirement is otherwise unchanged.
- **One layout, scaling difficulty.** Rather than authoring a new maze per level,
  later levels reuse the layout with faster and more numerous enemies. It is the
  simpler reading of "levels" and keeps the reachability guarantee trivially true
  for every level.
- **Squashing does not add an extra drop.** In the arcade original, an enemy
  caught under a falling ingredient rides it down and pushes it one girder
  further. Here a squash awards points and removes the enemy, and the ingredient
  falls exactly as far as it otherwise would. The bookkeeping for a ridden
  ingredient was not worth the complication.
- **No bonus items.** The original scatters coffee, ice cream and chips that
  refill pepper. Here pepper refills on a level clear only, which keeps the
  resource meaningful without adding a pickup system.
- **Enemies do not emerge from the plates.** They respawn at fixed points on the
  upper girders three seconds after being squashed.
- **The chef is never crushed** by a falling ingredient — only enemies are.
- **Stunned enemies are harmless.** Walking through a peppered enemy is safe,
  which is what makes pepper useful for escaping a corner.
- **A pile is stepped from the top.** When several ingredients share a girder the
  chef presses the topmost one, and dropping it takes the whole pile. This is what
  prevents a pile from ever becoming impossible to move.
