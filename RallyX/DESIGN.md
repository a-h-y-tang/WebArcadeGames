# Rally-X — Design

## Game concept

Rally-X is a top-down maze **driving** game. You drive a blue rally car through a
scrolling maze, collecting every yellow flag while red chase cars hunt you down.
The maze is much larger than the screen, so the view scrolls with the car and a
radar panel on the right shows the whole map: your car, the flags you still need
and the pursuers closing in.

Your only weapon is a **smoke screen**: dumping smoke burns fuel, but any chaser
that drives into the cloud spins out and sits helpless for a few seconds. Fuel is
finite and burns while you drive, so smoke is a resource to spend carefully —
running dry wrecks the car.

Nothing in the repo plays like this: `PacMan` and `DigDug` are single-screen maze
games with no scrolling and no vehicle handling, `RoadRacer`/`TurboRacer` are lane
racers, and `Maze` is a static puzzle. Rally-X adds a scrolling world, a radar
minimap, a fuel economy and a non-lethal weapon.

## Mechanics

### The maze

* The world is a `21 x 21` grid of `40px` tiles (`840 x 840` px). The visible
  canvas is `560 x 420`, so roughly a quarter of the world is on screen.
* Each level's maze is generated by randomised depth-first search over the odd
  cells (a perfect maze), then ~18% of the remaining interior walls are knocked
  out to create loops. Loops matter: a perfect maze is all dead ends, and dead
  ends against a chasing car are a death sentence.
* Generation is driven by a seeded `mulberry32` PRNG seeded from the level
  number, so level N always produces the same maze. That keeps the game fair
  across lives and makes the Playwright tests deterministic.

### Driving

* The car uses grid-locked, Pac-Man style steering: it always travels along a
  corridor axis at a constant speed. A requested direction is stored in
  `car.nextDir` and applied as soon as the car is close enough to a tile centre
  and the target tile is open — so you can pre-turn into a junction.
* The car stops (keeping its facing) when it noses into a wall.

### Flags

* Eight flags are scattered on open tiles, never within 4 tiles of the start.
* Driving over a flag collects it and scores 100 points.
* One randomly chosen flag is the **special flag** (drawn with an `S`). Once it
  is collected every *later* flag in that level is worth double. Collecting it
  last is worth nothing extra — the gamble is whether to detour for it early.
* Clearing every flag advances the level: score gets a bonus of `10 x remaining
  fuel`, the maze is regenerated, fuel is refilled and one more chase car joins
  (capped at 5).

### Fuel and smoke

* Fuel starts at 100 and burns at 2.2/second while the game runs.
* `Space` drops a smoke cloud at the car's tail for 6 fuel. Smoke lingers 3
  seconds. A chaser whose centre comes within the cloud radius spins out for 3
  seconds, during which it does not move and cannot hurt you.
* Below 25 fuel the gauge turns red and the HUD flashes a low-fuel warning.
* Fuel reaching 0 wrecks the car — same cost as being rammed.

### Chase cars

* Chasers drive tile-centre to tile-centre at a lower base speed than the
  player (100 vs 130 px/s), gaining 6 px/s per level.
* Each step the game floods the maze with a breadth-first distance field from
  the player's tile. On arriving at a tile centre a chaser steps to whichever
  open neighbour has the lowest distance, so it genuinely finds its way round
  corners instead of pressing itself into the wall nearest you. It refuses to
  reverse unless it has no other option, which stops the twitchy
  back-and-forth that naive pathing produces.
* Because chasers only re-decide at tile centres, cutting a corner tightly
  buys you distance — that, and the speed difference, is how you stay alive.
* Touching the player wrecks the car. You lose a life, the level's flags and
  fuel are kept, and the car and chasers are returned to their starting tiles.
* At 0 lives the run is over and the score is compared against the best score
  held in `localStorage` under `rally-x-best`.

## Controls

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| Arrow keys / `WASD` | Steer |
| `Space` | Start game / drop smoke screen |
| `P` | Pause / resume |
| Start button | Start or restart |

## Code layout

* `index.html` — HUD, the `560 x 420` game canvas, the `180 x 180` radar canvas
  and the overlay.
* `game.js` — everything else, written as plain top-level script globals (the
  convention every other game in this repo follows) so Playwright specs can call
  `step(dt)`, `startGame()`, `dropSmoke()` and read `car`, `enemies`, `flags`
  and `fuel` directly from the page.
* The simulation is a pure `step(dt)` function; `requestAnimationFrame` only
  supplies `dt` and calls `draw()`. Tests therefore drive the game by calling
  `step()` in a loop instead of waiting on real time, which makes them fast and
  free of flake.

## Assumptions

These are judgement calls made without being able to ask:

1. **Branch name.** The task asked for a branch named after the game
   (`rally-x`), but this session's standing instructions pin all work to the
   branch `claude/loving-euler-x1cjhf` and forbid pushing anywhere else. The
   session branch wins; no `rally-x` branch is created.
2. **Running out of fuel wrecks the car** (costing a life) rather than the
   arcade original's "car slows to a crawl". Losing a life is the simpler rule,
   is unambiguous to test, and keeps a stalled game from running forever.
3. **A wreck keeps the flags you already collected** and only sends the cars
   back to their starting tiles. The tank is refilled, because otherwise
   running dry would wreck the next car immediately and burn every life in a
   couple of seconds.
4. **The special flag doubles only flags collected after it**, which is the
   reading that makes the pickup order a real decision.
5. **One special flag per level**, chosen at random from the eight.
6. **Smoke is dropped behind the car** (one third of a tile back along its
   facing) so you cannot stun a chaser you are driving straight at.
7. **No sound.** No other game in the repo ships audio assets.
8. **Radar is a separate canvas** rather than an inset on the main canvas, so it
   can be styled and asserted on independently.
